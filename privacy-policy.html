YOUR PRIVACY, IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

Privacy policy.

Your habits are personal. Here’s what stays on your device, what settings sync, and the choices you control.

Last updated: November 10, 2026

Usage stays local. Settings can sync.

App Blocker Pro does not upload your app, website, or short-video usage history through settings sync. Google and online guest accounts use Firebase to store settings and reducer configurations.

01 / The short version

This policy covers App Blocker Pro for Android (package com.sahed.app_blocker_pro) and this companion website, provided by Sahed Alom Sumit (“we,” “us”). The app helps you understand screen time, block distractions, and apply focus rules.

The app processes usage and relevant screen information locally to provide the tools you enable. Account information and selected settings are processed through Google/Firebase for authentication and settings sync. We do not sell personal information or use your usage history for targeted advertising.

02 / Data on your device

Depending on the features and permissions you use, local app storage may contain:

  • Usage information: app identifiers, screen-time records, website/domain activity, short-video duration history, and focus-session statistics such as session totals, focus time, and streaks. The current app does not count reels or provide attention-span analytics.
  • Rules and preferences: selected apps and websites, keyword lists, time limits, schedules, unlock conditions and messages, Focus Groups, reducer configurations, and theme preferences.
  • Device-only information: permission state, protection passwords, active focus or Pomodoro sessions, and local diagnostic files where present. Settings edits apply immediately; the delayed-settings feature has been removed.

Android Usage Access supplies screen time through Android’s UsageStatsManager; a local tracker is used as a fallback when system events or permission are unavailable. Website and short-video details are processed on-device. Information visible through Accessibility is used to recognize supported interfaces and apply your chosen rules; it is not part of the settings-sync payload.

Backups and diagnostic files can contain sensitive information. If you export a backup or voluntarily send a file to support, that copy leaves the app’s private storage under your direction. Review it before sharing. The current app no longer includes the custom crash recorder or intent-log viewer.

03 / Google accounts, guests, and settings sync

You may continue with Google or start as a guest. Google sign-in uses Google Credential Manager and Firebase Authentication. Authentication may process your account identifier, name, email address, profile image, and authentication credentials or tokens needed to identify your account. The app does not receive your Google password.

Guest use works offline. When connected, a guest can receive an anonymous Firebase account. Both anonymous and Google accounts sync settings while online. Guest mode does not mean “no cloud data.” Linking Google helps keep those settings accessible across devices.

What sync includes

Firebase Cloud Firestore stores account-linked settings and reducer configurations. These configurations may include apps, websites, keywords, schedules, limits, and messages you choose as part of your rules. Although these are settings rather than usage history, they can reveal personal preferences.

What sync excludes

Usage history, local logs, active focus sessions, device permissions, protection passwords, and local pending-change deadlines are not uploaded by the settings-sync feature. Local changes can be queued offline and retried when connected. Android may delay background syncing.

Cloud settings are protected through Firebase authentication, account-based access rules, and the service’s transport and storage protections. This is not end-to-end encryption with a private passphrase. Account and service security depend in part on Google/Firebase and on protecting your device and Google account.

04 / Permissions and why they are used

  • Accessibility service: when enabled, the app can receive accessibility events and retrieve relevant screen content, such as app identifiers, visible interface text, browser addresses, or short-video elements. It uses these signals locally to detect distractions, enforce app/website/keyword rules, track supported short-video duration, and display blocking screens or configured unlock messages. This is a digital wellbeing tool, not an accessibility tool for people with disabilities. This is powerful access: enable it only if you are comfortable with these functions. You can disable the service in Android Accessibility Settings.
  • Usage Access: reads Android’s usage records to calculate and display screen time on this device.
  • Installed-app visibility: identifies installed apps so you can choose which apps to monitor or block.
  • Display over other apps: supports blocking, warning, or usage overlays where enabled.
  • Notifications and Do Not Disturb access: support the blocking service, focus timers, reminders, and optional Do Not Disturb during focus sessions.
  • Internet: supports authentication and Firebase settings sync. Network services can receive technical information such as IP address, connection details, and request metadata.
  • Foreground service, boot events, and vibration: support ongoing blocking, service recovery, and feedback.
  • NFC: supports optional tag-based unlock conditions on compatible devices. The current app does not request camera access; QR/barcode unlock challenges have been removed.

Permission availability and behavior vary by Android version and device. Revoking permissions may limit the related features. Website and short-video recognition depend on supported third-party interfaces, and Android battery restrictions can affect background operation. The app does not require root, device-owner access, or Shizuku to provide its ordinary accessibility-based blocking.

05 / Third-party services and sharing

Google Sign-In, Firebase Authentication, and Cloud Firestore provide account and settings-sync services. They process the account, settings, and technical service information needed to operate those features. The Android app also includes the Firebase Cloud Messaging library; Google/Firebase components may process installation identifiers or related technical metadata according to enabled service behavior. Usage history is not included in the app’s settings-sync upload.

See Google’s Privacy Policy and Firebase’s privacy and security information. Google Play, Android, and your device manufacturer may independently process information under their own policies. Service processing may take place outside your country.

If you contact us, we receive the email address, message, and any attachments you send, and use them to handle your request. Do not send passwords, authentication tokens, or sensitive browsing history. Information may also be disclosed where legally required or necessary to protect the security and lawful operation of the service.

06 / Privacy on this website

This is a static informational website. Its code does not set cookies, use browser storage, include analytics or advertising scripts, or collect data through a form. App installation and account deletion do not happen directly on this website.

The website loads DM Sans from Google Fonts. Your browser therefore contacts Google, which may receive your IP address and technical request information. See Google Fonts’ privacy information. The hosting provider may also process IP addresses, requested URLs, browser information, and timestamps to serve and secure the site. External links and email services follow their own policies.

07 / Retention, backups, and deletion

Local information remains until removed by app controls, cleared through Android Settings, or deleted when uninstalling, subject to Android and device backup behavior. The current app permits Android backup; whether data is backed up or restored depends on your device and Google backup settings. Exported backups remain wherever you saved them and must be removed separately.

Account-linked cloud settings remain until deleted. Signing out, clearing local storage, or uninstalling does not itself delete the Firebase account or cloud settings. Signing out retains local settings and the app continues as a guest.

Guests can use Settings → Account → Manage account and guest data → Delete guest data to delete guest cloud settings, the anonymous account, and local app data. A connection is required when a cloud guest account exists. For a Google-linked account, request account and cloud-settings deletion by email. See the full data-deletion guide, including retry instructions and the external request option.

We retain support correspondence only as needed to address requests and meet legitimate legal or security obligations. Deletion of live account records may not immediately remove provider-managed backups or legally required records; those remain subject to provider retention practices and applicable law.

08 / Your choices and rights

You can choose guest or Google sign-in, manage permissions in Android Settings, adjust or remove rules, manage device backups, and request account or data deletion. Depending on your location, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, or object to processing of personal information, and to complain to a relevant data-protection authority.

Contact us to exercise applicable rights. We may need to verify account ownership before acting. We cannot remotely retrieve or erase usage history stored only on your device, and we do not need that history to handle an account-deletion request.

09 / Children’s privacy

The service is not intended for children below the age at which they can consent to personal-data processing in their location. We do not knowingly collect account information from children below that age. If you believe a child has provided personal information, contact us so we can investigate and take appropriate action.
